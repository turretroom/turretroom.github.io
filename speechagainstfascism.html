Rally Against Fascism, 14 February 2026

I work for my living. There are other details to my life, but you’ve heard them all from a thousand other friends. I’ve cleaned up parks, I’ve written essays, I’ve been in bars, and I’m young. I graduated from college last year. I’ve seen a few things a young man shouldn’t see. I’ve seen some things that the ruling class has tried to prevent me from seeing. Even if the powers that be try their hardest to blot out my future with their war on our climate and their war on our freedoms, despite everything, I can still see the future.

When I think about the future, I think about the children. I can’t imagine a future without the children. I think the reason I can still get up in the morning is that I’m trying to act like there’s going to be a tomorrow, a next year, for them.

But I didn’t come here to teach you all. I didn’t come here to preach to you all. I only came to speak what I do know, and I have a lot to learn. The more I live, the more I see that. The longer I look into your eyes, the more I realize how vast people are. People are living history. The strength of people is the weight of history. The mind of the people is the sword of class struggle. But what moves me to act, what gets me out of bed in the morning, is what I like to call the heart of the people. The heart of the people sees justice. The heart of the people sees truth.

One tear shed by a working man for his brother is worth a thousand Artificial Intelligences, feigning human interest. A working woman’s beating heart is a thousand times stronger than the blood of any so-called master race. I do not heed the kings who lay claim to the halls of fame, nor the slave holders who wrote the narratives with their whips. I do not believe the sinners who tell me to dig my own grave and to carve the pathway to hell with my own bare hands. I will not build weapons for genocide. I will not build walls for apartheid. I will not repeat the devil’s lies. Never again. I have ripped the devil’s tongue out of my mouth, and if we have accomplished anything by bringing you all here today, it would be to inspire you to do the very same.

Whenever you tell yourself that the way you feel on Sunday, right before you go to work, is laziness, rip the devil’s tongue out of your mouth. Nobody who works for a living is lazy. Nobody. I wonder what would happen if you took everybody in this room and asked them how many hours they worked last week, how many years it would be. I wonder if any of you have ever been cut off at a union meeting, been disrespected by a pervert, insulted in front of your coworkers, or belittled in front of your boss. If so, you have a legitimate grievance that’s worth tearing the whole fucking place up over.

From here on out, if I hear anyone telling you to stay quiet over anything, or to just call a representative or a cop about it, I’m telling them to get behind me. Get behind me and get out of the way.

At the end of the day, we all agree that none of us can accept the fact that people do the work, but somebody else gets to take home the pay. The government’s going to rob you to fund ICE this year, then ICE is going to rub you of your neighbors. They rubbed you last year, and the year before that, to drop bombs on Palestinian families, hospitals, churches, and classrooms. The bills are coming for that, you know. Every goddamn year is looking more outrageous than the last, and the snow’s going to pile up outside so high you won’t even be able to smell the bodies anymore. You’ll have frostbite, and the doors to all the clinics will be locked. The doors to all the libraries will be locked. Your hometown parade is going to stop having the firefighters and the Little League teams show up. It’s just going to be cops, one after another, rolling down the street.

I wonder how many of you are going to be mowing your lawn and watching like it’s happening on TV. What kind of world are we letting our kids grow up in? This is the kind of thing my great-grandfather landed in Normandy on D-Day to stop. It took several enormous armies from both the east and the west, united, to defeat fascism and destroy it in World War II. What will it take today? The enemy today is the state. It’s the police. It’s the Department of Homeland Security. It’s the Department of Justice - so-called. It’s the state unions, it’s the Democrats, it’s the Republicans, it’s the Constitution itself! All of it!

That’s not the America I belong to. That’s not the America I was born a citizen of. I didn’t come here to talk to landlords, CEOs, and entrepreneurs. I’m not here on my hands and knees begging for scraps. I’m not talking to the city councilors. I’m not chatting and schmoozing with the politicians. I’m not talking to Trump. I’m talking to you. I’m talking to America, and I’m telling you: You are invincible. I’m telling you: Leave the talking to the rich and move quietly at first. Start by watching them, then move into position. Move that you may fight the enemy here and nail him in the jaw and bite him in the heel before he knows what hit him.

Fight to disarm the police. Fight to destroy their capabilities and their power. Fight to bathe in clean water again. Fight to eat fresh food again. Fight for your right to read and to speak what you will fight for: Peace of mind for security and for the general welfare.

Some of you may have taken off your name tags and uniforms three Fridays ago and gone on strike with the good people of Minneapolis. How did it feel to hold your chains in your hand? Were they so heavy? No, not at all. We have carried greater burdens, haven’t we? Civilize the mind and make savage the body, for nations are not made with metal alone. Test your will and your might against that of the enemy. This is the only way I can show you how powerful you really are.

I can tell you what’s ahead, but you have to take the step forward. My friends and fellow organizers who will speak and perform before and after me are delighted to introduce themselves to you. You’ll hear today from the Providence General Assembly, from the Democratic Socialists of Rhode Island, from the International Workers of the World. You’ll hear from CounterPower and the Providence Workers’ Defense. We are honored to welcome Daryl Lamont Jenkins and the One Peoples’ Project. My friends and I came here today to offer you a path forward for the revolutionary students in Providence and beyond.

I only have one more thing to say: I am confident in your intelligence, instincts, and willpower. I know that no matter which version of the future you decide to go with, the choice to move forward is yours. It can only be yours. Remember that and keep each other close.
